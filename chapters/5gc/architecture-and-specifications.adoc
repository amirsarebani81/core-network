== Architecture and Specifications

The 5G Core Network (5GC) is the core network of the 5G System (5GS).
TS 23.501 clause 4.2.3 describes the non-roaming 5GS architecture in two
equivalent representations: a service-based representation, in which
each control-plane network function (NF) exposes its services over a
service-based interface (SBI), and a reference point representation, in
which a named reference point connects each pair of NFs that interact.
This chapter shows the 5GC in both representations, with 3GPP access
through the Next Generation Radio Access Network (NG-RAN). Roaming
(TS 23.501 clause 4.2.4) and non-3GPP access (TS 23.501 clause 4.2.8)
are outside the scope of both figures.

=== Service-Based Representation

.5GC architecture, service-based representation
image::images/5gc-architecture-sba.svg[5GC Architecture, Service-Based Representation]

The figure above follows TS 23.501 figure 4.2.3-1 and attaches every
control-plane NF to the common service-based interconnect through the SBI
that the NF exposes, for example Namf for the AMF (TS 23.501 clause
4.2.6). Any NF can consume the services of any other NF over this
interconnect, so the figure shows which NFs are part of the 5GC rather
than which pairs of NFs interact. The NF services use HTTP/2 over TCP,
as TS 29.500 specifies for the technical realization of the service-based
architecture. The UE, the NG-RAN, and the DN do not expose an SBI and
therefore connect to the 5GC through the reference points N1, N2, N3, and
N6. The SMF controls the UPF over the N4 reference point; the Nupf SBI of
the UPF only serves specific exposure features (TS 23.501 clause 4.2.16)
and is not shown.

Compared with TS 23.501 figure 4.2.3-1, the figure adds the UDR and the
NWDAF, which NOTE 4 and NOTE 5 of TS 23.501 clause 4.2.3 leave out of the
TS 23.501 figures for clarity, and the UDSF, the CHF, and the 5G-EIR,
which TS 23.501 figure 4.2.3-1 does not show either. The figure leaves
out the EASDF, which TS 23.501 figure 4.2.3-1 shows, together with the
NFs of other specific features, for example the UCMF, the TSCTSF, the
DCCF, the MFAF, and the ADRF (TS 23.501 clause 4.2.2).

=== Reference Point Representation

.5GC architecture, reference point representation
image::images/5gc-architecture.svg[5GC Architecture, Reference Point Representation]

The figure above shows the same NFs as the service-based representation
and connects them with the reference points that TS 23.501 clause 4.2.7
defines between them. TS 23.501 figure 4.2.3-2 leaves out the NRF, the
NEF, the UDR, the UDSF, and the NWDAF for clarity (NOTE 2, NOTE 4, and
NOTE 5 of TS 23.501 clause 4.2.3) and does not show the CHF and the
5G-EIR, and TS 23.501 figure 4.2.3-5 shows the NEF separately; this
figure combines these NFs into one view.

The figure is arranged around the AMF and the SMF, which interact with
more NFs than any other NF. The centre column holds the NFs that both the
AMF and the SMF use, namely the UDM, the NEF, the PCF, the CHF, and the
NSACF. The AMF reaches each of these NFs over a reference point that rises
from the top of the AMF and turns right at the height of the target NF,
and the SMF reaches the same NFs over the mirrored reference points on the
right, so that each row of the centre column reads as one pair of
reference points, for example N8 and N10 for the UDM. The NFs that the
AMF uses and the SMF does not use sit to the left: the AUSF, the NSSAAF,
and the 5G-EIR to the left of the AMF, and the NSSF, together with the
NWDAF that serves the NSSF and the PCF, between the AMF and the centre
column. The NFs that only connect to the centre column, namely the UDR
and the AF, sit to its right. The bottom row holds the UE
and the user-plane path from the NG-RAN through the UPF to the DN.

The NRF, the SCP, and the UDSF are grouped in a frame because every NF
can interact with them. The NRF and the SCP have no reference point to a
specific NF in a non-roaming PLMN, apart from N116 between the SCP and the
NWDAF, and N18 connects any NF to the UDSF, so the figure does not draw
these reference points.

Next to each reference point, the figure gives the 3GPP technical
specification (TS) that defines the protocol on that reference point and
the name of the protocol. For a reference point between two control-plane
NFs, the TS is the stage 3 specification of the SBI that realizes the
reference point, and the protocol is HTTP/2. Where a reference point
crosses another reference point without a connection, the figure draws a
small arc.

=== Network Functions

UE (User Equipment):: The UE is the subscriber's device. The UE
communicates with the NG-RAN over the Uu radio interface and with the AMF
over N1, which carries the Non-Access Stratum (NAS) protocol of TS 24.501.

NG-RAN (Next Generation Radio Access Network):: The NG-RAN is the 5G radio
access network, made up of gNBs and ng-eNBs (TS 38.300). The NG-RAN
relays control-plane signaling to the AMF over N2 and user-plane traffic
to the UPF over N3, and NG-RAN nodes interconnect over Xn.

AMF (Access and Mobility Management Function):: The AMF terminates N1 and
N2 and handles registration, connection, reachability, and mobility
management for the UE, together with access authentication and
authorization (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.1). The AMF relays session management
messages between the UE and the SMF and exposes the Namf SBI (TS 29.518).

SMF (Session Management Function):: The SMF establishes, modifies, and
releases PDU sessions, allocates the UE IP address, and selects and
controls the UPF over N4 (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.2). The SMF exposes the
Nsmf SBI (TS 29.502).

UPF (User Plane Function):: The UPF is the anchor point of the PDU session
in the user plane. The UPF routes and forwards packets between the NG-RAN
and the DN and applies the packet detection, QoS, and usage reporting
rules that the SMF provisions over N4 (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.3). Two UPFs
interconnect over N9, for example when an intermediate UPF serves a PDU
session.

DN (Data Network):: The DN is the data network that the PDU session gives
access to, for example the internet, operator services, or third-party
services. The UPF connects to the DN over N6.

AUSF (Authentication Server Function):: The AUSF authenticates the UE for
3GPP access and for untrusted non-3GPP access (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.8).
The AUSF exposes the Nausf SBI (TS 29.509).

UDM (Unified Data Management):: The UDM generates the authentication
credentials, handles the user identification, and manages the
subscription data and the registration of the serving NFs for each UE
(TS 23.501 clause 6.2.7). The UDM can keep the subscription data in the
UDR and exposes the Nudm SBI (TS 29.503).

UDR (Unified Data Repository):: The UDR stores and retrieves the
subscription data for the UDM and the policy data for the PCF, together
with the structured data for exposure and the application data, for
example the Packet Flow Descriptions (PFDs) for application detection
(TS 23.501 clause 6.2.11). The UDR exposes the Nudr SBI (TS 29.504).

PCF (Policy Control Function):: The PCF provides the policy rules for
access and mobility management to the AMF and the policy and charging
control rules for the PDU session to the SMF (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.4).
The PCF exposes the Npcf SBI, whose services several stage 3
specifications define, for example TS 29.507, TS 29.512, and TS 29.514.

AF (Application Function):: The AF interacts with the 5GC to influence
traffic routing, to request policy control from the PCF, or to use the
exposure framework of the NEF (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.10). An AF in a
trusted domain can interact with the PCF directly over N5, and any other
AF uses the NEF.

NEF (Network Exposure Function):: The NEF exposes the capabilities and
events of the 5GC to the AF and translates the information that the AF
provides into requests towards the other NFs (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.5).
The NEF exposes the Nnef SBI, and TS 29.522 defines the northbound
application programming interfaces (APIs) of the NEF towards the AF.

NRF (Network Repository Function):: The NRF holds the profiles of the
available NF instances and their services, and it supports NF
registration and NF discovery (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.6). The NRF exposes
the Nnrf SBI (TS 29.510).

NSSF (Network Slice Selection Function):: The NSSF selects the set of
network slice instances that serve the UE and determines the allowed and
configured network slices (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.14). The NSSF exposes the
Nnssf SBI (TS 29.531).

NSSAAF (Network Slice-specific and SNPN Authentication and Authorization Function):: The NSSAAF
performs network slice-specific authentication and authorization with an
external authentication, authorization, and accounting (AAA) server, and
it supports access to a standalone non-public network (SNPN) with
credentials from a credentials holder (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.23). The
NSSAAF exposes the Nnssaaf SBI (TS 29.526).

NSACF (Network Slice Admission Control Function):: The NSACF monitors and
controls the number of registered UEs and the number of PDU sessions per
network slice (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.28). The NSACF exposes the Nnsacf SBI
(TS 29.536).

5G-EIR (5G Equipment Identity Register):: The 5G-EIR is an optional NF
that checks the status of the Permanent Equipment Identifier (PEI) of the
UE, for example to check that the PEI is not prohibited (TS 23.501 clause
6.2.15). The 5G-EIR exposes the N5g-eir SBI (TS 29.511).

CHF (Charging Function):: The CHF performs converged online and offline
charging for the charging data that the SMF and the AMF report (TS 32.240,
TS 32.255 for the SMF, and TS 32.256 for the AMF), and it provides the policy counters for spending limit
control to the PCF (TS 29.594). The CHF exposes the Nchf SBI (TS 32.291).

NWDAF (Network Data Analytics Function):: The NWDAF collects data from the
NFs, from the AF, and from operations, administration, and maintenance
(OAM) and provides analytics information to the NFs and to the AF
(TS 23.501 clause 6.2.18 and TS 23.288). The NWDAF exposes the Nnwdaf SBI
(TS 29.520).

UDSF (Unstructured Data Storage Function):: The UDSF is an optional NF in
which any NF can store and retrieve its unstructured data, for example UE
contexts (TS 23.501 clause 6.2.12 and clause 4.2.5). The UDSF exposes the
Nudsf SBI.

SCP (Service Communication Proxy):: The SCP is an optional NF that routes
the service requests between NFs for indirect communication and can
perform the NF discovery and selection on behalf of the consumer NF
(TS 23.501 clause 6.2.19 and Annex E). When an operator deploys no SCP,
the NFs communicate with each other directly.
